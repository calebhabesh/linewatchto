# Transit domain invariants

Read only the section affected by the task. These are behavior constraints;
feature requirements belong in the task/spec and implementation claims need code
and test evidence. Background inventory: [feature reference](feature-reference.md).

## Sources, freshness, and offline reads

- TTC and regional alert ingestion, arrivals, surface connections, train markers, schedules, and supplemental collections have independent enablement/freshness gates. Fresh alerts do not establish live arrivals or train markers.
- Preserve complete fixture fallback and source labels. Source timing must distinguish Started (`activePeriod.start`) from Updated (TTC `lastUpdated`) where published.
- Current-service cards show "Posted" only for unplanned incidents without a service window, never as a planned start. GO/UP uses its publication time; its `activePeriod.start` is a service-day boundary. TTC unplanned alerts start when published (observed within a poll interval), so their start is the posting time. Saved snapshots show the time without an elapsed age.
- Failed supplemental regional collections do not fail the entire poll or deactivate last-good rows; stale retained records still cannot drive fresh service claims.
- Offline public TTC and regional snapshots are separate, last-successful, and limited to seven days. Preserve timestamps and saved notices, mark current status unknown, suppress arrivals/markers/current account impact checks, and refresh on reconnect. The service worker never caches account pages or API responses.
- Public source use and website monitoring must meet [source launch gates](source-licensing-launch-gates.md). Station-page detection stages review candidates; it never automatically publishes or deactivates rider notices.

## Alerts, maps, and notices

- Ordinary delays and explicit Reduced Speed Zones are distinct. Schematic Diagram placements use reviewed topology and authored SVG geometry; Geographic map placements project the same logical topology and active impacts onto published GTFS track geometry and station coordinates, not inferred physical tracking. Estimated train markers are conservative schematic placements in both modes: on authored SVG corridor geometry in Diagram mode, and in Geographic map mode projected by the same segment progress onto the published track geometry of the marker's reviewed link. Neither is a physical train position.
- Preserve direction-aware segment matching and station rings. Station-only regional impacts must not become corridor-wide overlays: listed stops mark closures at each stop (ringed individually and labelled as a station list, including in push and commute notifications) unless exactly two stops bound a "between" span. "No train service on <route name>" closes the whole route.
- A GO/UP closure can close several separate sections of one line: each "no service between X and Y" clause is a section, and a closure notice that says only where trains still run ("GO trains will run between Mount Pleasant and Mount Dennis") closes the rest of its line from each end of that running section. Sections are stored as consecutive station pairs, ordered along the route with touching or overlapping ones merged; one event projects one impact whose segments cover every section, and the running section stays open. Its location names each section ("Union ↔ Mount Dennis and Mount Pleasant ↔ Stratford") in cards, push, and commute notices. Sections follow segment connectivity, so a branch (Lakeshore West's Hamilton GO Centre spur) is never inside a section that passes it.
- Rider-facing station spans state direction through their connector (`displayLocation`): `→` only when one direction is established and the stations are in travel order (TTC: every matched segment agrees; GO/UP: a named trip), `⇄` when all directions are affected (TTC bidirectional; GO/UP track, area, or closure notices), and a neutral `–` otherwise (no direction claimed; screen readers hear "to"). A GO/UP impact on every segment of its line names the line's terminals with the same connector rules and says "Entire Line" on its direction line. Source `location` text stays unchanged because push identity and history compare it.
- Display buckets are Suspensions, Delays, Planned Advisories, and Reduced Speed Zones. Keep service effect separate from scheduled/current timing: limited service is a delay-bucket restriction without implied delay minutes, frequency reduction, single tracking, shuttle service, or shutdown. Generic source causes are labelled source details, not effect evidence; contradictory structured effects remain unclassified.
- TTC scheduled advisories remain canonical planned events and project current impacts only during effective parent/child windows. Exact parent-period IDs link standalone active children; show one current map impact with access to the related planned advisory. Limited service uses delay styling while active and blue planned previews. A child's more specific evidence affects only its linked occurrence.
- An explicit TTC `ENDED EARLY` title status completes that closure or its exactly linked occurrence. Omit it from current impacts and planned listings, preserve later occurrences and source history, and retain completion evidence after the child leaves the feed. `ENDING EARLY` and service that "will end early" do not establish completion.
- Metrolinx can republish an unchanged notice under a new notice ID, often as a station-information copy of a line notice that lists the affected stops. Merge events whose lines, trips, effect, scope, title, body, active period, span, and impacts match, and whose structured stops are equal or absent on one side, into one event referencing every source record. The highest-priority source supplies its content, then the lowest notice ID; the event keeps the lowest notice ID so a later copy does not become a new event for push and history. Bodies are compared ignoring a repeated leading title, sentence spacing, and a final full stop, and rider text drops a repeated leading title. Differing wording or differing listed stops keep notices separate.
- Two supplementary notice kinds fold into the one event they describe, which keeps its own content and gains their source references: a listed-stations notice with the same line, effect, title (ignoring case), and active period whose stops all lie within one of a line notice's closed sections (for a no-service closure dated in both titles, the titles need only match without their dates and line name, since Metrolinx rewords station copies: "Kitchener Line Service Adjustments Oct. 31-Nov. 1"); and a `Top Red Banner` notice with the same line, effect, scope, and span whose known window overlaps a non-banner notice, and which states no trips, stations, replacement service, or delay that notice does not. A notice matching several events, or a banner without a known window, stays separate. Surviving closures or delays on a shared line with the same trips and overlapping windows and places are logged as possible duplicates, never merged; `scripts/audit-metrolinx-duplicates.sh` reports them and every consolidation reason locally.
- Metrolinx closes an incident with a new notice ID. An explicit resolution title status (`Final`, `Final update`, `Resolved`, `Regular service resumes`) completes it only when the body reports the resolution and no continuing impact: omit it from current impacts, overlays, commute matching, and push, while it stays readable in Service Notices. Possible residual effects make it an advisory. Resolution wording without that title status, an expired window, or a notice leaving the feed does not establish restoration.
- Optional model readings of Metrolinx notices (primary mode, the default when enabled) classify GO/UP rail notices; the deterministic classifier is the fallback. A new notice is read during the poll that first sees it, within a request limit and time budget, so its first stored version and any push use the reading. Unavailable, rate-limited, out-of-budget, invalid, or less-than-high-confidence readings leave the deterministic result; pending text is read later only while the notice is still in the feed and its window has not ended, and a later reading updates the event like any source update. A reading supplies the effect (no service, delay, or service adjustment), scope, span or listed stations, replacement, and skipped stops, validated against the line's catalog stations. It never changes dates, timing, delay minutes, or rider text, never overrides structured trip cancellations or explicit resolution notices, never moves sections the classifier derived from a stated running section, and cannot itself mark an incident resolved; a reading that cannot say where keeps the classifier's place. A `Train Cancellation` notice is a structured trip cancellation only when it lists trip numbers or names a timed trip; Metrolinx also files line closures under that category, and those classify from their text. Shadow mode only records differences. Classified events record the model in `fieldSources.modelReading`.
- Accessibility outages, surface notices, and reviewed station-page notices do not drive service status, segment overlays, commute matching, reliability incidents, or push. Station-page notices are station-detail-only. My Stations does not send push.
- TTC GTFS-RT service-alert supplementation is bus/streetcar-only; it does not drive rapid-transit map/status/commute/push paths.
- Explicit GO timetable announcements are corridor-tagged, deduplicated Service Notices. They do not imply a station span or drive current delays, overlays, commute impacts, reliability incidents, or push.
- Regional accessibility records omit restoration notices and unmapped/bus-only facilities; do not infer complete facility coverage, stable asset identity, platform matching, or guaranteed restoration.

## Arrivals and estimated train markers

- TTC live arrivals require the live provider, fresh Subway Trip Updates, and stop mapping through the active static GTFS import. Missing/stale directions or lines fall back to source-labeled schedules; no active schedule means unavailable, not invented service.
- GO/UP live arrivals require enabled regional arrivals, a backend-only key, and fresh mapped provider rows. Static schedules are published timetable times, not guaranteed departures. Distinguish no scheduled service from unavailable data.
- GO coach counts require exact trip-number matches to fresh in-service train records. UP does not invent coach counts.
- TTC surface connections join through published static-GTFS `parent_station`, never proximity. Stop departures (plan 13, S3) are read by the stop's published `stop_code`; a code missing from the active schedule says so rather than returning an empty stop. Regional surface connections expose GO Bus only. Bay/platform appears only if published. GO bus stop departures (plan 13, S6) are read from Metrolinx GO Next Service only for GO stop numbers in the generated GO bus stop list, under the same regional surface enablement, within a per-minute upstream budget.
- Surface routes and stops on maps: only the Map View, never a System Map, may draw TTC surface routes and stops, and only from published static GTFS. Surface routes and stops on the Map View are wayfinding layers: they carry no service status, and a drawn route never implies normal service. Streetcar geometry follows one schedule period's weekday streetcar trips; replacement-bus and short-turn trips are counted, not drawn, and a route with no streetcar trips is not drawn. TTC bus routes and stops (plan 13, S5) are drawn only behind the rider's Map View toggle (off by default), under the streetcars and subway, from the same period's weekday trips of daytime bus routes; night routes and short turns are not drawn. GO bus routes and stops (plan 13, S6) are drawn only on the GO/UP Map View behind their own toggle (off by default), under the GO/UP rail lines, from one full week of GO GTFS weekday trips; they never affect GO/UP rail status, and TTC sources never authorize GO bus claims (or the reverse). No GO bus vehicles are drawn. Surface reads and notices do not color, badge, or add overlays to surface routes; vehicles on maps need their own enabled, fresh source (plan 13, S4 and S8).
- Station floor plans (plan 13, S7) draw only on the TTC Map View, from the generated `StationFootprints_View` asset (never fetched at runtime), credited to the TTC. They are wayfinding: constant paint, never coloured or hidden by service data, and an elevator, escalator, stair or entrance drawn on a plan never implies it is working; accessibility outages remain their own source and never mark plan icons. Bus bays on plans come from published static GTFS station stops (never the plan source) and open that stop's departures under the stop departure rules.
- Live streetcar positions (plan 13, S4) and live TTC bus positions (S8) are GPS reports from their own enabled, fresh vehicle-position source, never placements inferred from trip updates or schedules; buses have their own flag over the same feed read, and neither flag enables the other. A car is shown only when its trip is a streetcar trip of the active published schedule (not a replacement bus); a bus only when its trip is a bus-route trip or a streetcar route's replacement-bus trip of that schedule (marked as a replacement), and only with the rider's bus routes on. A vehicle without a trip is not shown. Every car carries its report's age; a car whose report or source is past its stale limit shows as stale, never as live, and old reports are removed. A car or bus away from its route's drawn line is shown where it reports and flagged, not snapped to the line. Positions never color or badge routes or drive status, commutes, reliability, or push, and fresh trip updates or alerts do not make positions live.
- Each surface departure carries its status: live (fresh trip-update prediction) or scheduled (static GTFS). GO bus stop departures (S6) are live only when Metrolinx computed a time that differs from the schedule; a computed time equal to the schedule is scheduled, and a row without a scheduled time is not shown. Stop departures (plan 13, S3) mark it on every time shown (the arrival tile's live-signal or schedule icon, as station arrivals). A stale or disabled source shows that state, never old times as current.
- Estimated train markers are conservative schematic placements on reviewed adjacent links. TTC markers are suppressed during closed subway hours despite fresh Trip Updates. Regional markers require their own enabled/fresh keyed provider; UP also requires a matching direction-consistent TripUpdates trip. Omit ambiguous/contradictory records. A regional marker's progress along its link comes from the predicted arrival at its next stop in the same shared TripUpdates snapshot UP station arrivals read (GO and UP), so the marker reaches the station when its arrival tile says it is due; clients advance such timed markers by the clock between snapshots, never backward and never past the station until the feed reports the train there. Markers without a prediction keep a coarse status placement and are not advanced.

## Regional trip changes

- Operational GO exceptions/TripUpdates become public trip changes only with exactly one matching active static-GTFS trip and mapped stops. Unmatched operational rows remain internal.
- Fresh structured rider-alert cancellations may be shown unmatched with source labels; they must not invent times or coverage. Merge duplicate rider/exception/TripUpdates signals.
- Only schedule-backed supported changes annotate arrivals; only exact schedule-backed cancellations can match My Commutes. Do not imply equivalent UP trip-change coverage or reproduction of website notice prose.
- Cancellations stay outside status, overlays, delay counts, reliability incidents/duration, and commute travel-time estimates. Separate coverage-labeled cancellation analytics and filtered cancellation pushes are allowed. Expiry never means service restoration.

## My Commutes and push

- Preserve account ownership, full-path and direction-aware matching, independent outbound/return legs, and Toronto-time days/windows. Route edits preserve notification rules while recalculating paths/impacts.
- TTC timing uses active schedule median segment weights, then seeded travel times, then constant topology weights. Regional timing remains low-confidence topology planning. No cross-network routing, walking-transfer timing, accessibility-personalized matching, alternate-route recommendation, email delivery, or movement-based prediction is implied.
- Delay/RSZ extra-time ranges are bounded heuristics; suspensions/closures are unreliable, not fake detour durations. Cancellations do not alter travel-time estimates.
- Device push enablement differs from account preferences. Delivery requires permission, VAPID configuration, enabled push, relevant fresh network data, and matching stream/event/route rules.
- Commute cancellation pushes additionally require exact schedule matching, at least two ordered path stops in matching direction, the scheduled trip inside the selected leg schedule, and current time inside its notification window.
- Enabling/editing route rules silently baselines active impacts. Impacts beginning outside a window may notify once when the natural window opens. Planned/restored commute pushes respect the selected leg's open window; restored also requires its toggle and a prior active commute delivery.
- Preserve distinct update re-notification, unchanged-snapshot deduplication, separate active/restored lifecycle entries, high transport urgency, and non-silent persistent display requests. Browser policy can still remove notifications.
- Device reconnection catch-up is not route-rule baselining: it applies only to a replacement endpoint of the same installation whose enablement continued (browser rotation or push-service 404/410, never a rider's enable or disable), sends only still-active, uncleared alerts that became eligible while that installation was enabled, once per endpoint, and never an alert any endpoint of the installation, or any endpoint it continued, already displayed (the browser can report a new installation id with the new endpoint). Planned-advisory announcements are not caught up (reminders and started windows are). Two or more alerts, or any alert when the device also missed alerts that cleared meanwhile or announcements, go as one batch: each is shown silently and one summary alerts once, naming the outage and counting what was left out, with a tap to Alert History or Planned Advisories. A new endpoint waits 5 seconds and is then evaluated promptly, and the open page re-saves an endpoint it obtained 15 and 75 seconds later so one found dead on its first send is replaced while the app is open. Devices store and report endpoint hashes only.
- Planned-advisory follow-up policy is shared by commute and line streams (Smart, Within 24 Hours, Day Of, Announcements Only), emitting one applicable timing candidate per evaluation.

## Reliability and public claims

- Count service-impact intervals only where verified polling, GTFS-derived daily service spans, and applicable closure windows overlap. Distinguish unique impact time from additive incident-hours and polling coverage from schedule-date coverage; confidence uses the weaker coverage.
- Do not claim active analytics/telemetry without configured credentials and observed data, or implemented geometry/caching/capabilities without code and passing verification. Update README claims when behavior changes.
